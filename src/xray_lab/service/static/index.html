Plain-language guide

How to read this page

What this page does

A computer program looks at a chest X-ray and gives 18 numbers, one for each finding it was trained to recognise. You can pick one of the demo X-rays or upload your own. The numbers are produced fresh for every request.

This is a research demonstration. It is not a diagnosis, not a second opinion, and not approved for patient care.

How to read the numbers

Each score is between 0 and 1 and compares findings within this one image. A higher score means the model reacted more strongly. It does not mean an 80% chance of disease. The 18 scores do not sum to 1, and several can be high at once.

0 · weaker signal 1 · stronger signal

“Top output” is simply the highest of those 18 scores. It is not a confirmed answer.

The findings in plain English

Atelectasis
Part of a lung collapses or does not inflate fully.
Consolidation
Air spaces in the lung fill with fluid or cells, so an area looks solid.
Infiltration
A broad term for material in the lung where air should be.
Pneumothorax
Air leaks around a lung, which can partly collapse it.
Edema
Extra fluid in the lungs, often because of the heart.
Emphysema
Damaged lung tissue makes it harder to push air out.
Fibrosis
Scarring makes lung tissue stiff.
Effusion
Fluid collects in the space around a lung.
Pneumonia
Infection inflames the lungs and can fill air spaces.
Pleural Thickening
The lining around the lung becomes thicker.
Cardiomegaly
The heart looks larger than expected on the image.
Nodule
A small, rounded spot in the lung.
Mass
A larger rounded abnormality; it may or may not be harmful.
Hernia
Part of the abdomen pushes up through the diaphragm.
Lung Lesion
A general term for an abnormal area in the lung.
Fracture
A broken bone, such as a rib.
Lung Opacity
A whiter area where something is denser than air.
Enlarged Cardiomediastinum
The central chest area looks wider than expected.
No Finding
The original report mentioned no finding. It is not one of the model's 18 outputs.

Words used on this page

Model / checkpoint
A pattern-recognition network trained on many chest X-rays. We did not train it.
NIH label
A finding mined from the original radiology report. Reports and mining can be incomplete or wrong, so it is a reference, not perfect truth.
Sigmoid score
A 0–1 number used for ranking. It is not a calibrated probability.
Calibration
Whether a score of 0.8 really happens about 80% of the time. This demo has not been calibrated for a clinical population.
Grad-CAM
A heatmap built from the model's internal gradients. It shows where the maths is sensitive, not where a disease is.
DICOM
A medical image file containing pixels plus scanner metadata. The page applies the file's brightness window before scoring.

What it cannot tell you

  • It cannot rule a disease in or out.
  • It has not been compared with radiologists on a proper evaluation set.
  • The demo images come from a public dataset the model may have seen during training.
  • The heatmap is coarse and does not outline a lesion.
  • It does not account for your patient's history, symptoms, or other tests.
